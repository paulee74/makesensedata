How I work

Practical solutions start with understanding the problem.

I begin with the people, the business and the process before looking at the technology. Once I understand what you are trying to achieve, I can build something practical, refine it with you and help make sure it continues to provide value.

The starting point

You do not need to arrive with a technical specification.

Most organisations do not contact me because they have decided exactly which dashboard, workflow or data model they need.

They usually have a business problem.

Reporting may take too long. A regulatory deadline may be creating pressure. The business may have grown faster than its systems and reporting processes. Staff may be spending hours rebuilding the same information every month.

My first job is to understand what is actually happening, why it matters and what a useful outcome would look like.

The process

From first conversation to something people use.

The exact work varies, but the same principles apply throughout: listen properly, understand the detail, communicate clearly and build with the people who will use the result.

01

Listen

Understand what is causing difficulty.

I spend time with the initial sponsor and the people directly involved in the work. I want to understand the problem in their language and hear how it affects the organisation day to day.

02

Investigate

See how the process works today.

I review the existing reports, spreadsheets, systems and manual steps. This often reveals a difference between how a process is described and how it actually works in practice.

03

Define

Agree the scope, boundaries and outcome.

Before development begins, I set out what is included, what is not included, the assumptions being made and what a successful result should achieve.

04

Build

Create something tangible quickly.

I prefer to produce a useful first version rather than disappear for months. Once people can see and use something, the conversation becomes more specific and productive.

05

Refine

Improve it with the people who need it.

The first version creates a starting point. From there, we simplify, challenge, add, remove and improve until the solution answers the questions the business genuinely needs answered.

06

Support

Keep it useful as things change.

Reporting requirements rarely stand still. Ongoing support can help with refreshes, maintenance, changes, troubleshooting and further development.

Discovery

I need to understand more than the report.

A reporting problem can be technical, operational or organisational. Often it is a combination of all three.

During the early conversations, I am trying to understand:

  • What decisions are difficult today?
  • Which reports take too long to produce?
  • Where does the information come from?
  • Who prepares, checks and uses it?
  • Which steps are manual?
  • Where are the delays or repeated errors?
  • Are responsibilities and ownership clear?
  • Are stakeholders genuinely committed to the change?
  • What regulatory obligations need to be met?
  • What should the process look like when it works well?

Sometimes stakeholders appear engaged because they want a difficult problem to go away. That is different from being committed to changing the process and using the result.

Understanding that early helps avoid building something technically sound that the organisation is not ready to adopt.

Preparing the information

Most of the important work happens before the chart.

A dashboard may be the most visible part of a project, but it is rarely the most difficult part.

The quality of the final reporting depends on how the data is prepared, structured and defined.

Depending on the requirement, this may involve:

  • Combining information from several systems
  • Standardising dates, categories and reference values
  • Removing duplicates
  • Creating validation and exception checks
  • Agreeing consistent KPI definitions
  • Building repeatable transformations
  • Creating relationships between datasets
  • Reconciling figures back to trusted sources

The objective is not simply to make the data work once. It is to create a process that can be repeated and trusted.

Plain English

What the technical terms mean in practice.

The tools matter, but only because of what they allow the business to do.

Power BI

Interactive reporting that brings information together and lets users explore performance without rebuilding spreadsheets for every question.

Alteryx

A tool for automating repetitive data preparation, cleansing and transformation so processes that once took hours can be repeated consistently.

Data cleansing

Finding and correcting missing, duplicated, inconsistent or incorrect information before it is used for reporting or migration.

Data migration

Moving information from one system to another while preserving accuracy, applying agreed rules and checking that nothing important has been lost.

Automation

Replacing repeated manual tasks with a process that can run consistently, saving time and reducing the opportunity for human error.

Data model

A structured way of connecting different information so figures are calculated consistently and can be used across several reports.

The right fit

The project needs commitment from both sides.

The strongest results come when there is an engaged sponsor, operational teams are prepared to explain how the work is really done, and stakeholders are willing to adopt the change.

A technically strong solution cannot succeed if nobody owns it or the organisation is not prepared to change the process around it.

I work best with organisations that want to collaborate, share the detail and create something meaningful together.

Good reporting is not delivered to a business. It is built with the business.
