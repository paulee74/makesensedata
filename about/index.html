About Paul

Business understanding. Technical ability.

I sit between the business and technical worlds.

I can sit with stakeholders, understand the problem in their language and then work directly with the data to build a practical solution.

That combination has been shaped by years of work across healthcare, finance, regulatory reporting, operations, data cleansing, migration and business intelligence.

Why Make Sense Data

I kept seeing the same problems.

Over the years, I worked in very different organisations, but the reporting problems were often remarkably similar.

Businesses had useful information but struggled to access it. Teams spent too much time producing reports manually. Different departments worked from different versions of the truth.

Smaller and medium-sized organisations often needed better reporting but could not justify a full-time data team or a large consultancy project.

I realised there was room for a practical service that could understand the problem quickly, build something useful and help the organisation grow from there.

That became Make Sense Data.

The difference

I speak both languages.

Consultants often lean heavily towards one of two areas.

Some are very strong commercially but rely on technical teams to turn an idea into a working solution. Others are technically excellent but struggle to understand what the business genuinely needs.

My experience allows me to work across both.

I can understand the operational or regulatory challenge, speak with the people involved, challenge assumptions and then work directly with the systems, processes and data.

Good reporting starts with understanding the business, not opening the software.

A practical approach

Methodical, collaborative and straightforward.

I aim to get to the heart of the problem, define a clear scope and keep people involved throughout the work.

01

Understand what is really happening

I spend time with the initial sponsor and the people doing the work. I want to see the current process rather than rely only on how it has been described.

02

Look beyond the technical problem

Sometimes the solution involves improving data or connecting systems. At other times, it is about clarifying ownership, creating capacity or bringing the right people into the conversation.

03

Set a clear scope

The work, boundaries, assumptions and expected outcome should be understood before development begins.

04

Communicate and adapt

I work quickly and communicate openly. Where something unexpected changes the requirement, we revisit the scope and agree the right way forward.

RUNNING MY OWN BUSINESS

I know what it feels like to rely on good information.

Alongside my data work, I run Trail Monkey, a Jersey trail running events business.

That means I also sit on the other side of the table.

I use data to understand registrations, event demand, capacity, costs, marketing, pricing and long-term growth. The decisions I make affect the health of the business, so the information needs to be clear, reliable and useful.

Running Trail Monkey has reinforced the way I approach client work. Reports are not valuable because they look impressive. They are valuable because someone needs them to make a decision, save time or manage risk.

I am not building reports for the sake of building reports. I am building something somebody needs to use.

What success looks like

Useful work that continues to provide value.

I do not measure success by how technically impressive a report is. I measure it by what changes for the people and the organisation.

01

Less stress

A difficult report, deadline or process becomes more manageable and predictable.

02

Time back

Employees spend less time preparing information and more time using it.

03

Better decisions

People can find the information they need without wading through rows of data or several competing reports.

04

Still useful later

The strongest compliment is hearing that something built months or years ago is still being used.
