About Paul

I didn’t start in data. I started in operations.

I’m Paul Burrows, the person behind Make Sense Data.

For more than twenty years, I’ve worked across banking, consultancy, government and healthcare, helping organisations improve processes, reduce risk and make better decisions.

Long before I built dashboards, I was the person relying on reporting to understand what was happening and decide what to do next.

Today, I combine that practical business experience with Power BI, data engineering and automation to build reporting that people can trust and use.

Business understanding Technical expertise Practical delivery

What makes my approach different

Business understanding first. Technology second.

Many people can build Power BI dashboards.

What makes my approach different is the combination of technical expertise and practical business experience.

I’ve managed operational teams, interpreted complex regulations, redesigned processes, supported senior decision-makers and built reporting in environments where accuracy, traceability and judgement genuinely matter.

That means I don’t begin by asking:

“What dashboard do you want?”

I begin by asking:

“What decision are you trying to make?”

My journey

Different roles. One consistent theme.

Learning how organisations work, how decisions are made and how better information can improve the outcome.

01
2006–2015

Operations

Learning how businesses actually work.

My early career was spent in banking operations, treasury, securities controls and client services.

These were not reporting roles. They were operational roles, where deadlines, controls, customer outcomes and risk all mattered.

What it taught me: The numbers only make sense when you understand the work behind them.

02
2015–2017

Regulation, risk and process

Turning complex requirements into workable processes.

As international tax and reporting requirements changed, I became responsible for interpreting regulation, analysing customer data and designing compliant reporting processes.

The work included CRS, FATCA and US withholding-tax reporting, client onboarding, process mapping, documentation and operational controls.

What it taught me: Good reporting depends on clear definitions, robust processes and trusted data.

03
2018–2019

Business intelligence

Moving from producing reports to changing how they were used.

At Standard Bank, I moved fully into Business Intelligence, working with Trust, Investment and senior management teams.

Power BI and SQL reporting replaced extensive paper-based processes and gave managers a more complete, near real-time view of operational performance, client activity and financial information.

What it taught me: Reporting can fundamentally change how an organisation makes decisions.

04
2019–2021

Data and analytics consultancy

Applying the same thinking to very different problems.

As a consultant, I worked across HR, marketing, sales, operations and enterprise data migration.

Projects included Salesforce consolidation, Workday reporting, recruitment dashboards, salary and headcount analysis, marketing pipeline reporting and analytical solutions built against legacy AS400 systems.

What it taught me: The technology changes, but the starting point remains the same: understand the business problem first.

05
2021–2025

Government and healthcare

Building trusted reporting where the consequences matter.

My work within Government of Jersey and Health and Community Services included hospital operations, waiting lists, emergency demand, theatre utilisation, capacity planning and regulated pharmacy processes.

Alongside Power BI, I worked with SQL, data pipelines, Power Apps, Power Automate, Alteryx and governed analytical models.

What it taught me: Accuracy, governance and usability are not optional when reporting supports real operational decisions.

06
Today

Make Sense Data

Bringing business experience and technical expertise together.

Make Sense Data brings together everything I have learned from working in operations, regulation, consultancy, government and healthcare.

The objective is not simply to build dashboards. It is to help organisations understand what is happening, why it is happening and what they should do next.

The principle: The best dashboards are not built around data. They are built around decisions.

How I work

Start with the question.

I’m less interested in building complicated solutions for the sake of it. I want to understand what information is genuinely useful and build the simplest reliable way of making the answer visible.

01

Understand the business

Understand the organisation, its objectives, its processes and the decisions people are trying to make.

02

Understand the data

Identify where the information lives, assess its quality and agree the definitions that everyone will use.

03

Build the solution

Create a robust data model, meaningful measures and clear reporting built around real business questions.

04

Refine and improve

Test the reporting with the people using it and continue improving the solution as the organisation evolves.

Beyond the dashboards

I’ve also been the person making the decisions.

Alongside my data career, I run Trail Monkey Jersey, organising trail-running events across the island.

Running an events business involves budgeting, forecasting, marketing, customer service, logistics, risk management, supplier relationships and constant operational decision-making.

It gives me direct experience of the pressures business owners and managers face. Information has to be timely, practical and useful. A technically impressive dashboard that does not help someone make a decision has missed the point.
