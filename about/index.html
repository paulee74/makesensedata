About Make Sense Data

I like making complicated things simpler.

I’m Paul Burrows, the person behind Make Sense Data.

I’ve spent much of my career working with data, reporting and information — often in environments where there is plenty of data, but getting a useful answer from it is much harder than it should be.

Make Sense Data came from wanting to solve that problem in a practical way.

How I work

Start with the question.

I’m less interested in building complicated solutions for the sake of it.

I’d rather understand the question, work out what information is genuinely useful and build something that makes the answer easier to see.

That might mean a Power BI dashboard. It might mean automating a report that currently takes someone hours or days to produce. Sometimes it simply means bringing several messy sources together and establishing which numbers can actually be trusted.

What matters

Useful beats complicated.

Understand

Know what you’re trying to answer.

Good reporting starts with the decision or question, not with a collection of charts.

Simplify

Remove work that doesn’t need to be there.

If the same manual reporting process happens every week or every month, there is usually a better way.

Trust

Make the numbers dependable.

People should be able to use information confidently without first having to work out which version is right.

Experience

Built around real-world reporting.

My background is in data analysis and information, working with operational reporting, business intelligence and the systems behind the numbers.

That matters because a dashboard is only part of the job. The harder questions are often where the data came from, whether the definitions are consistent, how the reporting process works and whether people actually trust the result.

Make Sense Data brings those pieces together rather than treating reporting as a purely visual exercise.

Away from the spreadsheets

I also run Trail Monkey.

Alongside Make Sense Data, I run Trail Monkey, a Jersey trail-running events business.

Organising events has given me a slightly different perspective on data. When you are actually running something, information needs to be useful, timely and understandable — not theoretically perfect.

It’s probably also why I have a fairly practical approach to consultancy: understand the problem, solve it properly and don’t make it more complicated than it needs to be.
