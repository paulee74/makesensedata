Power BI case study

Emergency Department Analytics

An interactive Power BI reporting suite bringing Emergency Department demand, patient flow, waiting times and operational performance together so clinical and operational leaders can see where pressure is building and understand what is driving it.

The brief

Turn complex patient-flow data into useful operational insight.

Emergency Departments generate large volumes of operational and clinical information, but individual measures do not always show where pressure is building or why patient flow is slowing.

This Power BI model brings attendance, triage, clinician, arrival, outcome and waiting-time data together in one structured reporting suite.

Users can move from an executive view of demand and performance into the factors behind it, including triage, clinical workload, admission patterns, presenting complaints and length of stay.

Dynamic commentary responds to the current selection and explains the findings in plain language, helping users move from monitoring individual measures to understanding the operational context behind them.

What decision-makers can see

From headline performance to what is affecting patient flow.

Executive performance

Attendances, four-hour performance, waiting time to clinician, length of stay, admission rate, patients leaving before being seen and ambulance arrivals are brought together in one management view.

A demand heatmap shows how patient arrivals vary by hour and day, while monthly trends and presenting-complaint analysis provide context around changes in activity.

Operational performance & patient flow

Users can explore patients seen, admissions, transfers, discharges, triage performance and clinician workload in greater detail.

Waiting times, admission patterns, length of stay and clinician activity can be compared to help identify where pressure is developing and where further investigation may be needed.

How it was built

The key pieces.

Structured healthcare data model

Attendance, clinician, triage, complaint, arrival and outcome information are connected through a structured analytical model.

Dynamic operational measures

Measures covering attendances, admissions, waiting times, length of stay, four-hour performance, ambulance arrivals and patients leaving before being seen are calculated dynamically.

Patient-flow analysis

Demand heatmaps, waiting-time analysis, presenting complaints, admission patterns and clinician-level views provide different perspectives on how patients move through the department.

Dynamic commentary

Narrative measures respond to the current filters and summarise demand, patient flow and operational pressure in plain language.
