Retail & hospitality analytics

Understand what is driving performance across every location.

Island Brew is a fictional multi-site Jersey coffee business. This example brings sales, margins, targets, labour and waste together so managers can compare locations and understand the factors behind performance.

The challenge

Sales alone do not tell the whole story.

A busy location can generate strong revenue while still performing less efficiently because of higher labour costs, lower margins or greater waste.

Equally, a smaller location may generate less revenue but operate efficiently and contribute strong margins.

Looking at sales, staffing, product performance and waste separately makes those differences difficult to understand. Bringing them together creates a more useful management view.

The questions

Move from what happened to why it happened.

  • Which locations are generating the strongest sales?
  • Are stores meeting their sales targets?
  • Which locations produce the strongest gross margins?
  • How efficiently is labour being used?
  • Where is waste having the greatest impact?
  • Which product categories are driving revenue?
  • How does performance change by month, day of week or location?

Bringing the information together

One model, several parts of the business.

The value comes from connecting information that would otherwise be viewed separately. Once the measures use consistent definitions, managers can compare performance across locations with confidence.

01

Sales & transactions

Bring together net sales, transaction volumes and average basket value to understand demand and commercial performance.

02

Products & margin

Look beyond revenue to understand product mix and the gross margin being generated by different parts of the business.

03

Labour efficiency

Compare labour as a percentage of sales and sales generated per labour hour to understand operational efficiency.

04

Waste & targets

Monitor waste alongside sales targets so operational costs and commercial expectations are considered together.

Multi-site reporting

Use the same measures everywhere.

One of the difficulties in multi-site businesses is ensuring that each location is being assessed in the same way.

A consistent reporting model creates common definitions for measures such as sales, gross margin, labour efficiency and waste. This makes comparisons between locations more meaningful.

Managers can still investigate individual stores, but they are starting from one shared version of the information.

The wider opportunity

The dashboard is the end of the process, not the beginning.

Useful reporting starts by understanding where the information comes from, how it should fit together and which measures the business genuinely needs.

SOURCE

Operational systems

Sales, product, staffing, purchasing or other operational systems provide the underlying information.

PREPARE

Clean & combine

Information is checked, structured and brought together so common definitions can be applied consistently.

MODEL

Create useful measures

Sales, margin, labour efficiency, waste and targets are calculated in a way that can be reused across the report.

USE

Support decisions

The resulting reporting gives managers something they can explore regularly rather than rebuilding analysis each time.

The point

Better questions become easier to answer.

Instead of asking only how much a store sold, management can look at the relationship between revenue, product mix, margin, staffing and waste.

That makes it easier to identify where performance really differs and where further investigation may be useful.

The same approach can be applied to many businesses with multiple locations, teams, products or operational units.
