Power BI case study

Customer Service & Contact Centre Analytics

An interactive Power BI reporting suite bringing contact demand, service performance, customer outcomes and adviser activity together so managers can see where pressure is building, understand what is driving it and identify where attention is needed.

The brief

Turn operational data into something managers can act on.

Contact centres generate large volumes of operational information, but individual measures do not always explain why service levels are changing or where pressure is developing.

This Power BI model brings contact, channel, team, adviser and customer outcome data together in one structured reporting suite.

Managers can move from an executive view of demand, responsiveness, resolution and satisfaction into the teams, channels and advisers behind the result.

Dynamic commentary responds to the current selection and explains the findings in plain language, helping users move from monitoring individual measures to understanding what they mean.

What decision-makers can see

From headline service measures to what is driving them.

Executive operations

Contact volume, answer rate, speed of answer, abandonment, handling time, first-contact resolution and customer satisfaction are brought together in a concise management view.

A demand heatmap highlights busy days and times, while dynamic commentary summarises the most important operational and customer experience findings.

Operational performance

Managers can explore adviser workload, team performance, service-level achievement, handling time and customer outcomes in greater detail.

Team comparisons and adviser-level analysis make it easier to identify differences in workload, efficiency, resolution and satisfaction.

Business questions

Designed around the questions service managers need to answer.

01

Where is demand coming from?

Understand when contact volumes peak, which channels are generating workload and which contact reasons are creating the most demand.

02

Are customers getting the service they need?

Monitor response times, abandonment, first-contact resolution and customer satisfaction together rather than as isolated measures.

03

Where is performance different?

Compare teams and advisers to understand variations in workload, efficiency, resolution and customer outcomes.

04

Where should attention be focused?

Use filters, heatmaps and narrative insight to identify pressure points, coaching opportunities and areas requiring further investigation.

How it was built

The key pieces.

Structured operational model

Contact, date, team, adviser, channel and contact-reason data are connected through a structured analytical model.

Dynamic service measures

Contact volumes, answer rates, abandonment, response times, handling times, resolution and satisfaction are calculated dynamically.

Demand and team analysis

Heatmaps, team comparisons and adviser-level views help managers understand when demand occurs and how workload and performance differ across the operation.

Dynamic commentary

Narrative measures respond to filters and explain demand patterns, service performance, team comparisons and areas that may warrant further attention.

What this demonstrates

Operational reporting built around decisions, not just KPIs.

01

02
